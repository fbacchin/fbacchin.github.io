What Is My Company Actually Worth?

The fair value method for owners and CFOs of Italian and Swiss SMEs

The buyer will arrive with a number. If it's the only number in the room, it becomes the price.

Coming soon to the App Store for iPhone, iPad and Mac

Why arrive with a number of your own

Most owners find out what their company is worth from the person trying to buy it. By the time they realise the figure was an opening position rather than a valuation, the conversation has already been framed: from then on, every argument they make is an argument to move up from someone else's starting point.

FairValue exists so that doesn't happen to you. It follows the methods used in real company sales, explained for the person who owns the business rather than for the analyst: no jargon left unexplained, no theory that doesn't change what you would do.

What it does, in seven steps

From the figures in your accounts to a value range you can defend in front of a buyer, one step at a time.

  1. EBITDA and adjustments. The operating profit from your accounts, cleaned of one-off items, personal expenses and an owner's pay that is out of line with the market.
  2. Multiples. The value the market gives to similar companies, applied to your adjusted EBITDA.
  3. Discounted cash flow (DCF). The value of future cash flows, with a sensitivity table showing how it changes as your assumptions change.
  4. Asset value. What the company's assets are worth, as a floor for the valuation.
  5. Weak points and discounts. The weaknesses a buyer will use to push the price down, so you know them before they do.
  6. From enterprise value to equity value. Debt, cash and non-operating assets: what separates the value of the company from what belongs to the shareholders.
  7. Range and summary. Your value range and a one-page PDF summary to take to the table.

The app also includes a Guide in eight parts, explaining the reasoning behind every step.

Who it is for

  • Owners of SMEs, in Italy or Switzerland, who have started thinking about selling or have already been approached by a buyer.
  • Anyone who has heard a figure and doesn't know whether it is good or insultingly low.
  • Anyone whose accountant handles compliance, not deals.
  • Anyone who wants to tell the value of the company apart from the price they will receive.
  • CFOs and finance directors asked by the owner or the board to put a number on the company, with a method they can defend in a meeting.

Who it is not for

  • Large corporates and listed companies.
  • Anyone who needs a valuation for a court, a tax authority or a statutory purpose: FairValue is not a certified valuation and does not replace one.

Your numbers stay yours

FairValue works only on your iPhone, iPad or Mac. It asks for no account, has no server and sends your data to no one: accounts, adjustments and results stay on your device. The PDF summary is created there, and you share it, when you want and with whom you want.
