Two rulebooks, one button

The two federations have separate rules, and in a tournament it matters which one applies. The button at the top right switches between them: the name on the button is the rulebook you’re about to switch to, while the title at the top always tells you which one you’re reading right now.

An index that takes you to the page

The list of articles isn’t a decorative table of contents: each entry opens the PDF exactly at that article’s page. And the last line of the list always shows the edition you’re reading — the same one you see above.

It updates itself

When a federation publishes a new edition, the app notices within a day and downloads it, without going through the App Store. The index and the page references update along with the document, so they stay in step.

The rules are bundled with the app, so you can read them without a connection too — on a plane or where there’s no signal. The network is only needed to notice that a new edition is out. And with the share button you can send someone the full PDF of the rulebook you’re reading.

If something looks wrong

If an article opens the wrong page, or an edition looks out of date, three details are enough for a quick fix: which rulebook, which article, and the edition shown at the bottom of the list. The fix doesn’t require an app update.

To keep score during the game, there’s Burraco Score.

Free, and the ads can go

Codice di Gara Burraco is free, supported by ads: the ads can be removed with a purchase, and everything else is free for everyone. The old paid version is now called Codice di Gara Classic: it keeps working and keeps receiving new editions for those who already have it.
